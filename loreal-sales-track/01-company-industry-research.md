**What this means for us [R]:**
1. L'Oréal India is growing double-digit. Management will not accept a waste solution that slows growth. Revenue protection is a hard constraint, not a nice-to-have.

Garnier in a kirana, CeraVe at a chemist, Matrix in a salon and Lancôme at a department-store counter cannot share one intervention playbook. 3. L'Oréal already invests in central forecasting (Demand Sensing). Our solution must **complement** it at the last mile, not compete with it. In short, central forecasting sees warehouse demand, and the rep sees the shelf.

## 2. Channels: how beauty reaches the Indian consumer

| Channel | Role for beauty | Inventory/waste dynamics | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| **General trade (GT)**: kiranas, cosmetic and general stores, served by distributors on fixed beats | Still the backbone for mass hair care and skin care, sachets and small towns | Many small nodes, little system visibility, manual stock-keeping. Distributors carry weeks of cover. Most returns originate here. | [F] for GT importance (S17 summaries); waste concentration in GT is **[H]** |
| **Chemists/pharmacies** | Critical for dermo brands (CeraVe, La Roche-Posay) and part of mass skin care | Pharma-style batch discipline is more common. High velocity on hero SKUs. Good candidates to *receive* redistributed stock. | [H] |
| **Modern trade (MT)**: supermarkets and beauty chains | Assortment breadth, shade ranges, gifting | Usually centrally negotiated by key-account teams. Shelf-life acceptance norms at goods receipt mean near-expiry stock can't go here. | [F] MT growth (S18); acceptance-norm practice **[H]**, analogous to FSSAI food rule S24 |
| **E-commerce** (Nykaa, Amazon, Flipkart, D2C) | Discovery and premium; ~a quarter of BPC by some estimates | Central inventory, data-rich, not served by field reps | [F] S17 (estimates vary) |
| **Quick commerce** | Fastest-growing; L'Oréal's stated priority | Dark stores hold only ~3–4 days of cover (Dabur reference), so waste sits upstream. Shifts demand away from GT, leaving GT distributors overstocked. Accused of being a channel for dumping near-expiry stock. | [F] S7, S18, S19, S21 |
| **Salons** (Professional Products) | Back-bar (used in service) and retail (sold to client) | Back-bar is consumed, retail shelf sells slowly. Hair colour has a large shade tail. | [H] |

**Key market facts.**
- The size of India's BPC market varies by source: about US$28–33 bn in 2025, possibly ~US$40 bn by 2030 [F] S17. **Pick one source (Redseer or Euromonitor) and use it consistently.**
- E-com reached 18% of FMCG sales in the top-8 metros in Q4 2025, and q-comm is over ¾ of that. Kirana volumes grew only 1.1% [F] S18. This is FMCG-wide data, not beauty-specific.

**The channel-shift insight [H, strongly supported by S18/S19]:** as demand moves to q-comm and MT in metros, the same SKUs keep flowing into GT distributors under old norms. GT shelves slow down while sell-in plans assume history. This is a **structural** reason channel inventory ages *now*, not just bad luck. Dabur's Q2 FY25 correction is the public proof: it had to cut distributor stock from ~30 to ~21 days, and it guided to a quarter of revenue decline to do it [F] S19.

## 3. How FMCG/beauty field sales runs in India (generic model)

*This is how the industry typically works. **We have no access to L'Oréal India's internal processes**, so treat this as the frame to validate, not a description of L'Oréal.*

```
Company (factory/CFA) --primary sales / sell-in--> Distributor --secondary sales--> Retailer --tertiary sales / sell-out--> Consumer
```

- **Primary sales** are company invoices to the distributor (or super-stockist). These are what monthly and quarterly targets most often track.
- **Secondary sales** are distributor invoices to retailers, captured in the distributor's DMS. This is where SFA order-taking happens.
- **Tertiary sales or sell-out** are what the consumer actually buys. GT retailers rarely have POS data; MT and e-com do.
- **Field roles (typical):** distributor salesman (DSR) or company sales officer (TSO/TSE) runs beats. Above them sit ASM/ASE → RSM → Zonal Head → National Sales. Key-account managers handle MT, e-com and q-comm.
- **Typical tools:** an SFA app for order capture, beat plans, attendance and photos; a DMS for distributor billing, stock and claims. Indian vendors include Bizom and FieldAssist, which serve hundreds of CPG brands [F] S27. Retailer-direct ordering apps also exist (HUL Shikhar, 1.4 m retailers [F] S20).
- **Returns:** expired or damaged stock flows retailer → distributor → company and is settled through credit notes or replacement. This is a well-known source of disputes and cash-flow strain for distributors [F, grade C] S33; [F] S22, S23.

## 4. Why inventory goes bad in beauty (causes, from research and industry logic)

| Cause | Mechanism | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Sell-in pressure and month/quarter-end loading | Targets on primary sales push stock beyond what the shelf can sell; it surfaces months later as returns | [F] for the pattern in Indian pharma (S31) and FMCG schemes (S32); for L'Oréal **[H]** |
| Volume-based trade schemes | "Buy 10 cartons, get X%" moves stock into slow outlets | [F, grade C] S32 |
| Channel shift (q-comm, MT) | GT velocity falls while replenishment norms stay the same | [F] S18, S19 |
| Assortment mismatch | Premium serum in a value kirana; full shade range in a small store | [H] (industry-standard reasoning) |
| Shade/variant long tail | Makeup and hair colour have many low-velocity SKUs | [H] |
| New launches and pack changes | Pipeline fill for new products; old packs become unsellable in MT after a relaunch | [H] |
| Seasonality | Sunscreen, winter moisturisers, festive gifting packs | [H] |
| Poor FEFO/rotation | Newer stock sold first at distributor or outlet; old batch expires at the back | [F, grade C] S30 |
| Handling damage | Heat (lipsticks, creams), crushed cartons, leaking pumps, tester misuse | [H] |
| Weak demand activation | Product sits behind the counter, no tester, no advocacy | [H] |

## 5. Existing systems: what exists, and the gap

| Capability | Exists in the market? | Gap relevant to this case |
|---|---|---|
| Order capture, beat plans, GPS attendance (SFA) | Yes, mature (Bizom, FieldAssist, Salesforce Consumer Goods Cloud, SAP) [F] S27 | Built to maximise *orders*, not sell-through |

| Retailer self-ordering apps | Yes (Shikhar) [F] S20 | Adoption and dormancy challenges [F] S20; no expiry logic |
| Image-recognition shelf audit | Yes (ParallelDots, Infilect) [F, grade C] S28 | Measures share of shelf and availability. Doesn't see expiry dates or back-room cartons. Costly per image. |
| Central demand forecasting | Yes, L'Oréal has "Demand Sensing" [F] S11 | Forecasts flows into distributors, not stuck stock at outlet level |
| **Batch-level, outlet-level risk detection → prescribed field action → tracked outcome** | **No public evidence of a packaged product** (S27 searches) | **This is our white space.** We present it as "not found publicly", not as "doesn't exist". |

**Implication [R]:** L'Oréal India almost certainly already has an SFA/DMS. **We should not pitch a replacement.** We pitch an operating model plus a module ("SMARTSELL") that can sit inside whatever SFA L'Oréal runs, with a standalone prototype to prove the logic.

## 6. Sustainability context (why this matters to L'Oréal beyond the P&L)

- **L'Oréal for the Future** (2020) sets 2030 targets on climate, water, biodiversity and resources, including 100% of industrial-site waste recycled or reused [F] S12.
- **Packaging:** 33% recycled packaging components by weight and 44% recycled or bio-based materials in 2025, against a 50% target for 2030. Trade press says some 2025 packaging goals were missed [F] S13, S14.
- **India:** 100% renewable energy across plants (Chakan, Baddi) and offices, and 100% EPR plastic collection (tonnage equivalent) [F] S15.
- **Unsold products:** L'Oréal has worked since 2020 on recycling finished goods that "cannot be sold or donated" [F] S16. Ordering the options as **sell, donate, recycle** gives us the hierarchy for our end-of-life playbook.

**The link we make [R]:** an expired unit is the most wasteful unit L'Oréal makes. It carries all the embodied impact (ingredients, packaging, manufacturing, forward transport, A&P) and adds reverse logistics and destruction. **Preventing expiry is upstream of recycling.** It is waste avoidance at the top of the waste hierarchy, which no packaging initiative can achieve.

> Caution: we found **no public L'Oréal number** for the carbon or packaging footprint of returned or expired goods. Any sustainability figure in our deck must be illustrative and labelled (see `09-financial-model.md`, section 6).
